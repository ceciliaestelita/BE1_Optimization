Deriving by hand, the process to find the gradient and the Hessian Matrix is demostrated below: 

 $ f(x,y) = \frac{1}{2}(x-1)^2 +\frac{1}{2}(10(y-x^2))^2 + \frac{1}{2}y^2 $
\newline
Simplifying: 

 $ f(x,y) = \frac{1}{2}(x-1)^2 +50(y-x^2)^2 + \frac{1}{2}y^2 $
\newline
\\
Gradient:
\[
\nabla f(x,y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\\\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

Deriving each part of $ f(x,y)$ in terms of x:
\newline

    $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\frac{1}{2}(x-1)^2) = x-1$
\newline

By the chainrule:

    $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(50(y-x^2)^2) = 50*2(y-x^2)(-2x)$
\newline

    $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\frac{1}{2}y^2) = 0$
\newline

Which sums up to:
\[
{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,y) = 200x^3-200xy+x-1}
\]
\newline

Now deriving each part of $ f(x,y)$ in terms of y:
\newline

    $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(\frac{1}{2}(x-1)^2) = 0$
\newline

    $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(50(y-x^2)^2) = 100(y-x^2)$
\newline

    $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(\frac{1}{2}y^2) = y$
\newline

Which sums up to:
\[
{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,y) = -100x^2+101y}
\]

\[
\nabla f(x,y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
200x^3-200xy+x-1\\\\
-100x^2+101y
\end{pmatrix}
\]
\newline
\\A similar process is taken to obtain the Hessian matrix:
\[
H(x,y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
fxx&fxy\\\\
fyx&fyy
\end{pmatrix}
\]
$fxx=1-200y+600x^2;\\fxy=-200x;\\fyx=-200x;\\  fyy=101.$

\[
H(x,y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
1-200y+600x^2&-200x\\\\
-200x&101
\end{pmatrix}
\]
